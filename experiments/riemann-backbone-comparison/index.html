03 / Mechanism to test

A speed hypothesis, not a speed result

M2 removes repeated relative-edge steerable lifts from the later layers. That may reduce geometry-dependent work, but kernels, attention, graph construction, memory traffic, and optimization can dominate wall time.

Expected from design

Less repeated edge-kernel work in M2

M2 should need fewer steerable edge-kernel applications than M1 at the same graph and depth. This is a computational mechanism, not a guaranteed end-to-end speedup.

Must be measured

Both faster training and inference

Compare training step time, time to matched validation quality, and model-only plus full-policy latency on the same GPU. M1 versus B0 has no assumed ordering.

Must not be lost

Equivariance and task quality

Check type-0/type-1 residuals, pose error, and paired closed-loop success. A faster but less effective policy does not validate the original objective.

04 / Personal experiment report

RiEMann B0 / M1 / M2 study

Planned / no measurements

Research question. Can our SO(3)-equivariant backbone make the RiEMann manipulation policy faster in both training and inference, without materially reducing pose accuracy or task success?

Identification strategy. Reproduce B0, then replace only the three network backbones. All three candidates use the same full-scene XYZ/RGB input, radius graphs, ROI protocol, typed heads, pose construction, losses, data split, and execution stack.

Models and provenance

B0 is the official RiEMann SE(3)-Transformer policy. M1 is a proposed layerwise relative-edge equivariant Transformer using our SO(3) representation machinery. M2 is a proposed one-time relative-edge lift followed by nodewise equivariant Q/K/V and local invariant attention. Our existing ConvE3 is nodewise in its current call pattern; M1 requires a genuine edge-message adapter. Neither M1 nor M2 is presented here as already trained.

Frozen comparison protocol

Audit repository commits, data hashes, GPU/software versions, and the paper-versus-code training mismatch before choosing a single executable configuration. Start with official Mug data and a trajectory-level split; then run paired SAPIEN scenes when simulation assets and success rules are available. Use at least three training seeds. Compare 2,500-point public-code preprocessing and a separate, uniformly processed 8,192-point extension. Do not compare a new measurement directly with the paper's A40 timing.

Measurement contract

Report forward, backward, optimizer-step, samples/s, peak memory, and wall time to a preregistered validation threshold. At batch 1, report warm-up-and-synchronized p50/p95 times for φ, graph/ROI, ψ1, ψ2, pose construction, model-only, and the complete policy. Report translation and rotation errors plus paired T/NI/NP/DO/ALL simulation success with confidence intervals and failure categories.

Decision rule

The dual-speed claim requires faster training to matched quality and lower complete-policy inference latency on the same hardware, while pose quality and task success meet thresholds fixed before final testing. A reduction in theoretical edge operations alone is not sufficient. If one metric fails, report the partial outcome rather than averaging it away.
